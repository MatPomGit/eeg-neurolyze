# Research publication policy

## Submission-first publication policy (author directive, 2026-11-07)

**Submit a scientifically defensible, finished manuscript rather than leave it unused solely because a journal might reject it.** When the evidence supports the claims, the work meets scientific and editorial standards, ethical and legal requirements are satisfied, and the author has explicitly approved submission, prioritize a timely attempt at an appropriate journal over indefinite polishing. Rejection is an acceptable editorial outcome: assess the feedback, revise as warranted, and propose another suitable journal.

Publication points are relevant to the author. A realistic possibility of earning even a modest number of points (for example, **20 Polish evaluation points**) is preferable to shelving a complete manuscript that will earn none. Verify the journal's **current** applicable classification, points, indexing, fees and fit; never claim acceptance or points are guaranteed. Do not treat submitting a paper as equivalent to receiving points.

### Operational decision rule

1. **Ready to submit:** evidence-to-claim alignment, a defensible contribution, adequate methods and reporting, journal fit, research integrity and required clearances are established. Complete submission files, assess venue options, and present a concise author approval decision without avoidable delay.
2. **Limited weaknesses:** address bounded, fixable issues or narrow unsupported claims; do not indefinitely delay a valid, more focused manuscript in pursuit of a hypothetical higher-tier venue.
3. **Material blockers:** do not submit with fabricated or unsupported results, unresolved mandatory ethics/consent or licensing issues, fundamental methodological defects, or missing author authorization. Record specific blockers and the shortest feasible remediation path.
4. **Rejected manuscript:** record the decision, triage actionable reviewer/editor feedback, identify an alternative journal, and prepare a revised submission. Rejection is not a reason to abandon sound work automatically.

**Authority boundary:** Agents may autonomously prepare analyses, manuscripts, checklists and recommendations, but must not submit a manuscript, contact editors/publishers on behalf of the author, approve charges, or commit publication funds without the author's explicit approval. Existing project safety, independent review and reproducibility requirements remain in force.
